The table now knows how to show it

Where the mod stands today against Foundry VTT, TaleSpire and the game feel of Baldur's Gate 3 — with what was verified in the real code, not in the feature list one would like to have.

scope: core + dungeon addon + species addon base: SRD 5.1 compared against: Foundry VTT · TaleSpire · Baldur's Gate 3
what changed since the first reading

The three highest-impact points of the previous round are already in the mod: a visual combat HUD (initiative board, conditions, action economy — it replaced the plain text), a persistent active effects panel (conditions, concentration, temporary HP, outside combat too), and the two most-cited BG3 mechanics, high-ground advantage and shoving. The two most expensive items of the original list remain: configurable triggers and fog of war — neither has been touched yet.

01

The real asset: rules that resolve themselves

Before comparing against anyone, it is worth saying what is there: it is not a pretty character sheet glued onto Minecraft. It is a 5e rules engine that really runs — rolls, conditions, spell slots, resistances, all automatic — with Minecraft as the board.

✦ already works on its own
  • Attack, damage, advantage/disadvantage, critical hits, saves and 14 SRD conditions with a real mechanical effect (not just an icon the DM has to remember what it does)
  • Concentration, death saves, spell slots with upcasting by level
  • Initiative that starts on its own with the first blow — nobody types "let's roll initiative"
  • Legendary actions, multiattack, "own-clock" monsters (they act every 6s without waiting in a queue)
  • Resistance/immunity to magical and non-magical damage, cover, area shapes (cone/line/sphere/wall) that come out of the right point
  • New — an always-visible initiative board (TurnHudOverlay), with conditions per combatant, who has already acted and your own action economy
  • New — a persistent active effects panel (ResourceHudOverlay): conditions, concentration, temporary HP, outside combat too
  • New — high-ground advantage and Shove, the BG3 tactical combination (throwing someone off a ledge) is now really possible
  • New — surfaces (SurfaceManager): a fire impact leaves the ground burning for a few rounds, and the world's REAL water puts it out or conducts a lightning spark to whoever is standing in it
✕ not yet
  • No macros or scripting for a DM to automate something without recompiling Java
  • Vision by light/darkness, not by line of sight behind a wall
  • No unified content search — each type (spell, monster, class) has its own separate screen
02

Against Foundry VTT: built-in automation, not module automation

Foundry's 5e system, as it comes, is more a digital character sheet than a referee — the attack/condition/resource automation the community takes for granted only arrives with modules like Midi-QOL and DAE on top. This mod brings that out of the box, with nothing else to install.

What Foundry still wins is everything built around it over years of modules: a macro editor accessible without programming, compendia searchable by free text, and lighting/vision that computes real occlusion against walls drawn on the scene. Its "active effects" now has an equivalent of its own (see above) — of the three original points, that is the one that closed. The other two are still UI/engine work that hasn't been done yet, not a design limitation.

03

Against TaleSpire: the terrain is already playable, hiding it is what is missing

TaleSpire sells a 3D table with miniatures — the mod already has a genuine analogue in the dungeon addon (jigsaw piece pools, capturing your own build as a reusable piece, tracing in the world) and it is more flexible in one specific sense: any build by a player can become reusable content, you don't have to limit yourself to a fixed asset library.

TaleSpire hides the table beyond each miniature's camera range — real fog of war. Minecraft has no such native concept, and it fights against how the game loads the world, not against a missing screen: it was the most expensive gap of the three comparisons. Decision: suspended. The mod already has a vision system of its own (VisionManager — light cutoffs, darkvision, blindness) that answers the same table question ("what can my character see right now?") without needing to hide the whole world behind it; building fog of war on top would mean maintaining two systems for the same problem. If at some point the map has to be hidden explicitly (not just what a character sees), this is picked up again — until then, the existing vision is the answer.

04

Against Baldur's Gate 3: the game feel, not the script

BG3 is a single campaign with cinematic cameras, voices and companions with relationships of their own — chasing that 1 to 1 is the wrong goal for a mod designed for a human DM to run their own table. What is transferable is how BG3 shows combat.

resolved since the first reading

The turn HUD is no longer loose lines of text: TurnHudOverlay shows the whole initiative board (order, conditions, who acted) and your own action economy as a state, not as a number lost in the chat, with the same visual treatment as the rest of the mod's screens. AttackRules.advantageAgainst now adds high-ground advantage, and ShoveManager resolves Shove as a real contested check — the "shove them from above" combination that defined half of this point now works.

All five of the original list are already in. The version of surfaces that was built uses the world's REAL water instead of tracking a separate second "water surface" — Minecraft already knows where water is, so putting out fire and conducting lightning query the real block. What remains pending is BG3's other iconic combo, fire+oil, because there is no content in the bestiary/spells that today leaves a pool of oil — it is a content decision, not an engine one, so it can be added the day it is needed without touching SurfaceManager.

06

Where to go next

Of the five original points, three are already done (visual HUD, effects panel, surfaces — see above) and one is suspended on purpose. Only one is really left open.

JSON-configurable triggersonly one pending
Not a full scripting language — a bounded format ("if X happens, apply Y") that gives the DM some of the power of a Foundry macro without asking them to compile Java. It needs a design decision before writing code: which kind of trigger is worth more (monster reactions like "on reaching 0 HP, do X"? something more general for homebrew content?).
suspended until further notice

Fog of war. It was the most expensive of the original options, and with the existing vision (VisionManager) covering the same table question, maintaining two systems for the same thing is not worth it. Off the work list — it is not picked up again unless a concrete need appears that the current vision doesn't solve.

Based on a direct reading of the code in this session — CombatManager, TurnManager, TurnHudOverlay, ResourceHudOverlay, AttackRules, ShoveManager, SurfaceManager, VisionManager, and the dungeon addon. It includes nothing that was not verified in the repository.
